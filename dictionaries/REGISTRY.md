# Dictionary registry

SPEC 12.2 and decision 20.9. Every dictionary has an entry here with its ID, version, SHA-256, corpus description, reproducible training command (zstd version, parameters, seed) and license. Dictionaries are CC0 only, are never deleted, and are released only when signed by at least two keyholders. New dictionaries arrive by pull request with held-out measurements, and the pull request reviews the corpus sources below.

**Status of this file: no dictionary has been released.** The three files under `eval/` are evaluation artifacts produced by `sim/cairn_sim/dict_eval.py` to measure the compression gains asked for by the v0.2 roadmap (SPEC 16.2). They are **not released dictionaries**: they carry no signatures, no version a decoder may rely on, and may be replaced or deleted before a release. A released dictionary must be retrained from a reviewed corpus, signed by two keyholders with published keys, and only then is it permanent.

## Corpus rules

* Training material is public domain, CC0, or covered by a rights-holder CC0 waiver. A dictionary contains literal fragments of its training data, so a source that is merely "free to read" is not enough.
* Private messages are never used. Captured traffic is allowed only for beacons, bulletins and broadcast traffic meant for redistribution. The project has captured none yet, so none is in the corpus.
* ARRL bulletins and ARES material are excluded (no waiver). Winlink traffic, mailing-list posts, amateur-authored forms and any third-party exercise material are excluded.
* Downloaded text is ASCII-folded, stripped of page furniture, e-mail addresses and `@` handles, and cut into 100-500 byte messages by `dict_eval`. Telephone numbers that appear in NWS boilerplate (public offices) are left as published.

## Reproducible training command

```sh
uv sync --package cairn-sim                       # python-zstandard 0.25.0 (libzstd 1.5.7), pinned in sim/pyproject.toml
uv run --package cairn-sim python -m cairn_sim.dict_eval --write-dicts
```

* Samples: the 80% training split, as messages of 100-500 bytes. Natural text is split by document (per source, the 20% of documents with the smallest `SHA-256("20261107:" + file name)` are held out; the eCFR part is cut into its sections first), then cut at word boundaries into messages of a uniformly drawn size (seeded by the file name). Synthetic messages come from `random.Random(20261107)`, a seeded shuffle (`20261108`) holds out every fifth. Natural and synthetic samples are mixed in a shuffle seeded `20261109`, in that order.
* Trainer: `zstandard.train_dictionary(size, samples, k=0, d=8, f=20, split_point=0.75, accel=1, steps=40, level=3, threads=1, dict_id=0)`, which runs libzstd's `ZDICT_optimizeTrainFromBuffer_fastCover`, for `size` in 32768, 65536, 114688.
* Seed: libzstd's trainer takes no random seed; with a single thread and identical samples in identical order it is deterministic (checked by `sim/tests/test_dict_eval.py`, and by separate runs producing identical files). The seeds above fix the corpus split, the chunk sizes, the synthetic messages and the sample order.
* Compression used for the measurements: zstd level 19 with the dictionary, frames without checksum, content size or in-frame dictionary ID.
