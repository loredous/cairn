.DISCUSSION... Issued at 117 PM EST Sat Nov 7 2026

Lake effect clouds amid cyclonic flow departing the eastern Great Lakes are showing some signs of breaking up. Rising heights and a subtle backing of the low-level flow should slowly chip away the cloud deck for at least a couple peeks of sun late this afternoon. Decreasing clouds set us up for, in the context of this mild fall, a very cold night. Lows will tumble toward freezing (away from the lake) as high pressure settles over Illinois. Light wind to start the evening may set the stage for frost, but then surface wind increases prior to daybreak in response to a low pressure streaking across Ontario. We're now past the climatological growing season; therefore, no frost/freeze headlines.

Breezy but dry on Sunday as the aformentioned low passes to our north. Forecast soundings show strong flow in the low-levels but poor mixing to the surface. This keeps wind gusts in check, generally from the west at 25 to 30 mph. Cool northwest flow behind this dry frontal passage restarts lake effect clouds which persist into Monday morning.

Broad northwest flow through the Great Lakes offers times of breezy conditions for the week ahead, while a building ridge over the Central US results in a slight warming trend through Wednesday afternoon. Given limited moisture transport, little to no rain is in the forecast. Our best chance for any rainfall is not until late Wednesday and Thursday when a fairly deep trough approaches. Even so, this may be a glancing blow as low pressure from the Mississippi Valley favors rain toward the Ohio Valley, shunted south by an incoming Great Lakes trough. This trough sets up lake effect precipitation, primarily west of our area.
