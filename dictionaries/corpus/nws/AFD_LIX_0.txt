.SHORT TERM... (Now through Wednesday) Issued at 1119 PM CST Sat Nov 7 2026

Split upper flow continues this evening. Northern stream shortwaves were near New York City and entering northwest Minnesota. A deeper northern stream trough off the Washington coast is moving toward ridging over the northern Rockies. The southern stream shortwave that was near Las Vegas last night is now over northern Mexico to the south of the Arizona-New Mexico border. Under mostly clear skies tonight, there's a wide range of temperatures at 11 PM CST, from 43 at Pascagoula to 64 in New Orleans.

Surface high pressure near the Missouri-Arkansas border this evening will drift slowly southward toward the northern Gulf Coast through the first half of the week. The southern stream shortwave will move across the local area Tuesday night or Wednesday. Precipitable water values struggle to get above 1 inch (right at the daily mean), and low level RH values don't get much higher than 50 percent. I suppose we could get some very light precipitation out of a mid level cloud deck as the shortwave moves across the area, but it's not going to amount to much, and for now, the forecast will remain dry.

Most high temperatures should be in the 70s through Wednesday, but won't be surprised if a few spots make it to 80 or 81 if cloud cover is limited. Overnight lows should be in the 40s to around 50, with the exception being the southshore of Lake Pontchartrain, where warmer water nearby will probably hold lows in the 50s.

.LONG TERM... (Wednesday night through Saturday) Issued at 1119 PM CST Sat Nov 7 2026

Medium range models trending into decent agreement in carving out a pretty deep trough over the Great Lakes, upper Ohio River Valley and eastern third of the country for the second half of the workweek. The lead northern stream shortwave will impact the local area Wednesday night or Thursday morning. If any significant rain was going to occur over the next week or two, that appears to be the only real opportunity, and probably just near the coast. The GFS has been more bullish on precipitation, with the ECMWF operational dry. Currently, the NBM PoP numbers are tracking with the ECMWF solution...no rain. The front associated with the shortwave will definitely bring cooler and dry air into the area for Friday and Saturday.

Highs Friday and Saturday are expected to top out in the 60s, and some guidance isn't even that warm in southwest Mississippi. With dew points forecast to drop into the 30s Friday and Saturday, that brings at least some potential for patchy frost in normally colder locations like the Pearl and Pascagoula River basins on Saturday morning if winds decouple. That looks to be the coldest night with lows in the 30s across the north half of the area. There's at least a limited chance that some well sheltered locations could get pretty close to freezing. Friday and Sunday mornings are a few degrees warmer than Saturday morning. Temperatures look like they'll rebound to above normal by day 8/9 (Sunday or Monday).
