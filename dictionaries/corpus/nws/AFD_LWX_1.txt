.DISCUSSION... KEY MESSAGE 1...Dry and seasonable this weekend.

High pressure will build near and south of the region through the weekend as an upper trough axis pulls away to the east. Brisk west to northwest winds will usher in cooler, more seasonable temperatures. Winds will gradually diminish this evening, except at higher elevations and over the waters. Sunday will still be breezy, but about 5-10 mph lower.

Wave clouds/stratocu likely persist west of I-81 into the evening. It will be mainly clear and dry otherwise.

The central Virginia piedmont will be closer to the high and have a chance to decouple (winds going light/calm) tonight and again Sunday night. Near freezing temperatures are expected as a result. Frost/freeze headlines have been issued, and if current guidance is correct, the growing season may end tonight for several zones in central Virginia.

KEY MESSAGE 2...Dry and mild Monday through Wednesday.

One dry front will cross by Monday, leading to breezy northwest winds returning. Cold advection behind this front is modest, so high temperatures will likely be similar to Sunday when combined with downsloping effects.

Another dry/weak front will move across the region Tuesday. Temperatures will be about 5 to 8 degrees above average as there could be a warming surface wind out of the southwest. Highs will reach the middle 60s to lower 70s with upper 50s to near 60 in the mountains. Lows Tuesday night will drop into the upper 30s to mid 40s. Once the front moves through, a surface high pressure will quickly build into and across the region for Wednesday. This will allow for temperatures to remain above average by a few degrees and the day to be dry. Highs in the middle to upper 60s with middle 50s in the mountains. The high quickly moves east by Wednesday night. Lows mid 30s to mid 40s. The colder temperatures could support some frost/freeze concerns where the growing season continues mainly east of the Blue Ridge.

KEY MESSAGE 3...Colder and unsettled Thursday and Friday.

Looking into the Thursday and Friday timeframe, an area of low pressure could move along the Ohio Valley or Lower Great Lakes and push into parts of the Mid-Atlantic region. An associated cold front will trail the main low. Some rain showers could accompany the low and front. During the same timeframe, a secondary low pressure system (or multiple smaller low pressure systems) will try to catch up to the first low and thus intensify it near the Eastern Seaboard. There are some discrepancies with the many models in the position, timing, and intensity of the secondary low pressure system. As for now, there is a higher confidence that the weather could become unsettled with rain showers or periods of rain, as well as some higher elevation snow or rain/snow mix. It is too early to tell where or to what extent any snow will evolve in any areas further to the east and southeast in our region. Highs Thursday only in the upper 50s to lower 60s and Friday will have highs in the upper 40s to middle 50s with some mid/upper 30s for highs in the Appalachians.
