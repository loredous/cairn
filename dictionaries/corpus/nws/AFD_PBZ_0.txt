.DISCUSSION... KEY MESSAGE 1... An exiting closed upper low today, followed by a swift-moving trough late Sunday into Monday morning, may drive periods of lake-enhanced light rain showers for portions of northwest PA. Additional tightening of the surface gradient with the second wave yield increased afternoon wind gusts that may see portions of eastern Tucker County approach Wind Advisory criteria.

Outside of these weather features, the prevailing outlook through midweek sees height rises and building high pressure that promotes dry weather and temperature rising about 5 to 10 degrees above normal by Tuesday.

KEY MESSAGE 2... Ensemble model guidance has fairly high certainty in the development of the next upper level trough starting Wednesday with a lobe of energy digging south out of Canada. But starting Wednesday night and beyond, large variances in the development/positioning of this system is resulting in notable swings in potential outcomes. For example, by Saturday, current ensemble cluster analysis suggest that the upper trough axis could either be exiting the Atlantic Coast, OR an nearly cutoff low sits over the eastern Tennessee River Valley. Until closer alignment of outcomes is seen, little actionable weather assessments can be provided.

The broad strokes of what can be said is the transition to an active pattern Thursday through at least Saturday that may include multiple periods of precipitation while likely pushing area temperature below normal. The latter point also means that scenarios do exist for the first accumulating snow of the season, though mainly for northwest PA and the higher terrain.
